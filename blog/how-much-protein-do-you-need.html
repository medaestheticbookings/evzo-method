How much protein do you actually need?

Πόση πρωτεΐνη χρειάζεσαι πραγματικά;

There are two honest answers to this, and the gap between them is where most of the confusion lives: one number keeps you healthy, the other helps you hold muscle while you lose weight. They are not the same number and nobody ever says which one they mean.

The floor, and the useful amount

The official minimum across the EU and most national guidelines sits around 0.8 g of protein per kilogram of bodyweight per day. That is a floor — the amount at which a sedentary adult does not develop a deficiency. It is not a target, and it is routinely quoted as if it were.

The range used in practice for someone training and trying to change their body composition is roughly 1.6 to 2.2 g per kilogram. Above that, the research stops showing much extra benefit. For an 80 kg person that is about 128 to 176 g a day, against a floor of 64 g.

If you are losing weight, the higher end matters more, not less. In a calorie deficit your body will take some of what it needs from muscle unless you give it a reason not to. Protein and resistance training are that reason.

Why this is hard in a Greek kitchen

Not because the food is bad — because the structure of the day is. A typical Greek or Cypriot day front-loads carbohydrate and back-loads protein: coffee and something from the bakery, a light lunch, then most of the day's protein arriving at nine in the evening.

Total protein matters most, but spreading it across the day is easier to hit and easier to hold. Three meals of 35 g is a far more achievable structure than one meal of 100 g and two of nothing.

What 30 grams actually looks like

  • 120 g of chicken breast
  • A 250 g tub of strained Greek yoghurt
  • Four eggs, or two eggs plus 150 g of egg whites
  • A 160 g tin of tuna
  • 130 g of halloumi — which also carries about 33 g of fat, so it is not free
  • A tin of chickpeas plus a slice of wholemeal bread

Notice that the last one needs two foods. Pulses are good protein and they are not dense protein: getting to 30 g from chickpeas alone means eating close to 350 g of them.

Do you need powder?

No. It is food in a tub, and it is convenient rather than magic. A scoop is typically 30 g of powder carrying somewhere around 24 g of protein — roughly one tin of tuna, for a similar price, without the tuna. Use it if it makes the day easier to hit. Do not use it to rescue a day that had no protein in it at all.

The practical version

Pick a protein for every meal before you decide anything else about that meal. That single habit does more than any calculation, because the meals that go wrong are almost never the ones where you chose the protein first.
